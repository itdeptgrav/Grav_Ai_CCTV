Both NVRs are CP PLUS (Dahua-based) CP-UNR-4K4322 recorders and expose the SAME three
proven paths: (1) the vendor RTSP playback URL with start/end date-time, (2) the vendor
HTTP recording-search API, (3) ONVIF Profile G (recording search + replay URI). Seek,
pause and 2x / 4x fast-forward work inside one RTSP session. Recorded audio is present.
The URL was not guessed: the path is the NVR's own (ONVIF GetReplayUri answers
rtsp://<nvr>/cam/playback?token=...), and the channel / starttime / endtime form (the
vendor's Dahua-style playback parameters) is proven by the NVR's replies -- 200 with the
requested duration, 404 when nothing is recorded -- and by the camera's burned-in clock
(OSD) in the decoded frames. Every claim below is backed that way.

==========================================================================================
WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PLAYBACK FEATURE (facts to design with -- nothing built yet)
==========================================================================================
 1. Time zones: the URL starttime/endtime are NVR LOCAL time (IST); RTSP clock= ranges
    and ONVIF times are UTC. Both NVRs are IST without DST, so the conversion is a fixed
    5:30 -- the browser's time must be converted to IST, not sent as-is.
 2. NVR clocks are 35-40 s slow (NTP is off on both). Footage is labelled with the NVR
    clock: "10:00:00" on the NVR happened at about 10:00:40 real time. Recommendation
    (your decision, nothing changed): enable NTP on both NVRs.
 3. Only the MAIN stream is recorded (subtype=1 -> 404; ONVIF lists "Extra" recording
    tracks but they are empty). Every playback stream is 2560x1440 H.265 at 25 fps,
    ~3.3-3.5 Mbit/s. Decoding it costs about as much CPU as a live "Original" tile; that,
    not the NVR, will limit how many cameras can play at once on the server.
 4. The NVR paces playback at real speed (1x) -- it does not send faster when read
    faster. Smooth 2x/4x or slow motion is not available from the NVR; Scale 2/4 gives
    key-frame-only fast-forward (about 1 frame per 2 s of footage).
 5. Seek: use PLAY Range clock=<UTC> (exact wall time, correct across recording gaps);
    npt counts recorded time only, so it drifts after a gap. Pause: PAUSE / PLAY.
 6. Availability: DESCRIBE answers 200 + recorded duration or 404 (works wherever RTSP
    works, i.e. also from outside the office); mediaFileFind lists the actual files and
    their exact start/end (finds gaps), but only on the office LAN.
 7. Remote access: the router forwards only the RTSP ports, so from outside the office
    the RTSP playback URL + DESCRIBE are usable, the vendor HTTP API and ONVIF are not.
    Production (Alienware) is on the LAN and can use all three.
 8. Security observation: both configured NVR accounts are in the "admin" group with
    full rights (user management, system config, backup, ...). Playback only needs the
    "Replay" (and "Monitor") rights; a dedicated least-privilege account would limit the
    damage if the credentials leak. Nothing was changed.
