1. ROOT CAUSE (this is what "measure first" found — the original guess was wrong)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The slowness was NOT simply "too many connections at once." Two hard facts,
proven by measurement, explain all of it:

(A) OpenCV's FFmpeg backend has a HARDCODED ~30 000 ms open/interrupt timeout.
    The env option we were passing, OPENCV_FFMPEG_CAPTURE_OPTIONS="...stimeout;
    5000000", is IGNORED for the open phase in this build. Proof — opening a dead
    channel with every timeout option variant still blocked exactly ~30 s:
        stimeout;3000000    -> 30.05 s   (log: "Stream timeout ... after 30003 ms")
        timeout;3000000     -> 30.05 s
        rw_timeout;3000000  -> 30.02 s
        cap.set(OPEN_TIMEOUT) after a blank VideoCapture() -> 30.09 s (no-op)
    A single dead/absent channel therefore froze for 30 s.

(B) OpenCV's FFmpeg opens are SERIALIZED PROCESS-WIDE. Opening 6 cameras "at
    once" does not actually open them in parallel — they go one at a time. Proof —
    with NO connect limit, 6 threads opening simultaneously still went live in
    strict ~2.5 s steps (8.6 -> 11.0 -> 13.5 -> 16.2 -> 18.6 s), and a per-NVR
    "connect limit" of 1 vs 2 vs 3 made NO difference to first-frame time:
        connect limit 0 : first healthy 8.65 s | all 18.62 s
        connect limit 2 : first healthy 8.61 s | all 18.52 s
        connect limit 3 : first healthy 8.50 s | all 18.13 s

Consequence of (A)+(B): because opens are serialized, ONE dead channel taking 30 s
blocked EVERY healthy camera behind it. That is why all six tiles sat on
"Connecting..." together. The originally-proposed "connect limiter" could not fix
this (opens were already serialized); the real levers are the open timeout and
the ordering of the serialized queue.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FIX (what changed in server.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HONORED open/read timeout. Pass CAP_PROP_OPEN_TIMEOUT_MSEC /
   CAP_PROP_READ_TIMEOUT_MSEC as VideoCapture CONSTRUCTOR params — the ONLY form
   this build honors. Dead channel now fails in ~8 s, not ~30 s. (Verified: a
   blank-VideoCapture()-then-cap.set() is a no-op; constructor params work:
   dead channel failed in 3.11 s at open_to=3000, 8.03 s at open_to=8000.)
     -> chosen value 8000 ms: MUST stay above the slowest HEALTHY cold open. An
        NVR2 substream needs up to ~7 s on its first (cold) connect; at 6000 ms a
        healthy camera ("Floor 9 - Cabin") was wrongly killed and wasted 2 retries
        (~20 s). 8000 ms fixed that while still capping a dead channel at ~8 s.
        Raising this does NOT slow a real camera — a camera that is there opens
        when the NVR answers (~2.5-4.5 s); the timeout only bounds a dead one.

2. GLOBAL CONNECT GATE + FRESH-FIRST ORDERING. Since opens are serialized anyway,
   a single global gate makes it explicit and lets us ORDER the queue. A camera
   whose previous open FAILED yields the gate until every not-recently-failed
   camera waiting has gone first — the requested "failed camera goes to the back
   of the queue; healthy cameras are not delayed behind a dead channel." A
   process-wide recently-failed registry (60 s) means a channel known-dead from a
   prior page view yields on its very first attempt too, so revisiting a page is
   fast. A camera that opens successfully is cleared from the registry at once.

3. PROGRESSIVE BACKOFF, NO SLOT/GATE HELD DURING BACKOFF. A dead channel retries
   at 1,2,3,4,5 s (capped) and holds NO NVR slot and NO gate while backing off.

4. JPEG-ENCODE-ONCE. Each decoded frame is encoded to JPEG a single time in the
   camera worker and shared to all viewers as immutable bytes. N browser viewers
   of one camera now cost ONE encode, not N. (Was: encode per viewer per frame.)

5. CAP_PROP_BUFFERSIZE=1 so reads return the newest frame (lower latency).

Kept intact (NOT undone): NVR active-stream cap = 6 (CCTV_NVR_MAX_CONN), the
viewer-count lifecycle, per-NVR slot release on every attempt, the fixed-<img>
grid, the page-change teardown, the fullscreen slot handoff. No extra worker
threads were added; NVR_MAX_CONN is unchanged.

3. SINGLE-CAMERA BASELINE (open cost is NVR handshake latency, not our code)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                         RTSP open   first-frame   TOTAL     resolution
  NVR1 ch3 (substream)   ~2.4-2.5 s   +0.02 s      ~2.5 s    352x288
  NVR2 ch1 (substream)   ~4.0-4.4 s   +0.05 s      ~4.1 s    1280x720

  - First frame arrives almost instantly AFTER open (0.02-0.06 s). The entire
    cost is the RTSP open handshake, which is NVR/network latency we cannot
    reduce from the client. FFmpeg probesize/analyzeduration/nobuffer/low_delay
    tuning was tested and made NO difference (NVR1 2.43->2.31->2.39 s;
    NVR2 4.14->4.22->4.35 s), confirming the cost is the handshake, not decoding.
  - The grid already uses the SUBSTREAM (subtype=1) — verified in nvr_config.py.
  - NVR1 substreams (352x288) open ~2 s faster than NVR2 (1280x720). NVR2's
    higher substream resolution is the reason NVR2 pages start slower.

     immediately and healthy cameras lead.                               PASS

HONEST LIMITATIONS (not hidden):
  - Because opens are serialized process-wide by OpenCV, a full 6-camera page
    cannot all appear at once. Total "all up" time = sum of the opens: ~15 s for
    6 NVR1 cameras, ~27 s for 6 NVR2 cameras. This is a hard OpenCV/FFmpeg limit,
    not fixable without a per-camera subprocess architecture (out of scope, and
    the user asked not to over-engineer / not to add threads).
  - On the FIRST cold encounter of a dead channel, if its worker wins the initial
    open race it still costs ONE ~8 s timeout before the first healthy camera.
    We cannot know a channel is dead before trying it. After that first failure
    it is remembered and deprioritised, so it is a one-time cost per channel.
  - A single healthy camera is ~2.5-4.5 s; sub-3 s is achievable for NVR1 pages
    but NVR2's ~4.5 s open is an NVR-side floor we cannot beat from the client.

10. FOLLOW-UP: "some cameras never come / stuck on Waiting for NVR slot" on the
    later pages (2-5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: on pages 2/3/4/5, only 1-2 tiles showed video; others sat on
"Connecting...", "Waiting for NVR slot..." or flickered in and out.

Diagnosed with /api/status + a direct channel probe (map_channels.py) and a
concurrency test (test_stability.py). THREE distinct causes, all now fixed:

(1) DEAD-CHANNEL SLOT STARVATION -> "Waiting for NVR slot...".
    A dead/absent channel held one of the NVR's 6 slots for the whole ~8 s of its
    (failing) open, so on a page with 1-2 dead channels the working cameras were
    starved of slots, especially during page hand-offs.
    FIX: take the NVR slot AFTER a successful open, not before -- a failing open
    now never occupies a slot. Only real live streams count against the cap.
    RESULT: pages 2/3/4 show every working camera; "Waiting for NVR slot" is gone.

(2) NETWORK-MONITOR FLAPPING -> healthy cameras dropped to "CCTV NETWORK
    UNREACHABLE". The monitor did a single 1 s TCP check every 5 s; over the
    PUBLIC path one jittery check flipped a healthy NVR to "down" and gated its
    live cameras.
    FIX: hysteresis -- an NVR is only declared down after 3 consecutive failed
    checks (~15 s), and the check timeout was raised to 2 s (CCTV_NETWORK_TIMEOUT).
    RESULT: zero false "unreachable" over sustained polling.

(3) DEAD-CHANNEL CHURN + wasted CPU -> live streams flickered.
    Dead channels retried every ~5 s; each 8 s open hogged the serialized connect
    gate, so a healthy stream that briefly dropped could not re-open for 15-25 s.
    Separately, each worker JPEG-encoded EVERY decoded frame (~20-25 fps) though
    only 8 fps is served -- needless CPU that starved the reads.
    FIX: exponential backoff for repeatedly-failing channels (1,2,4,8,16,30 s cap)
    so a dead channel stops hammering the NVR/gate; and decode every frame but
    ENCODE only at the serve rate (STREAM_FPS).
    RESULT: page 4 went from 1-2 flickering to a steady 5 LIVE; server CPU for 5
    live cameras dropped from ~1.2 cores to ~0.46 core; memory ~266 MB.

GROUND TRUTH (map_channels.py, direct open of every channel): 7 of 25 channels
were dead or intermittent at test time -- nvr2 ch4,7,12,13 and nvr1 ch7,10,12
(several are flaky rather than fully dead and recover on their own). These show
