 1. EXACT REASON                                                           FOUND, FIXED
    The NVR does NOT use the RTP channel the client asks for. The audio-only SETUP asked
    "interleaved=0-1"; the NVR answered
        Transport: RTP/AVP/TCP;unicast;interleaved=2-3;ssrc=...
    (it numbers the channels by track: audio = track 1 -> 2-3). The audio RTP then
    arrived on channel 2 (RTCP on 3), while the client kept reading channel 0 -- the
    channel it had ASKED for. So it counted 0 audio packets, hit its read timeout
    ("READ_TIMEOUT: no audio packet for ~5 s"), reconnected, and did that forever:
    "Audio reconnecting..." with no sound (your server had 43 such sessions on corridor
    office, all with 0 packets). Both NVRs behave this way (NVR1 also answers 2-3).
    The offline tests passed because the fake NVR echoed the requested channel.
    Fix: rtsp_audio.py reads interleaved= from every SETUP reply and uses THOSE channels
    (audio RTP, RTCP, and the video track's in the fallback). The fake NVR now assigns
    its own channels like the real one, and a test reproduces the old failure.

==========================================================================================
3. ARCHITECTURE CHOSEN                                                    PASS (offline + browser)
==========================================================================================
  CURRENT : OpenCV receives each camera's RTSP session. FFmpeg inside it even receives
            the audio track, but OpenCV cannot hand audio out (proven, see summary).
  OPTION A: separate audio RTSP connection per camera          <- chosen, ON DEMAND ONLY
  OPTION B: one ingest (own RTSP client / FFmpeg) for video+audio, fanned out
            -> the better long-term design IF audio is used a lot, but it means
               replacing OpenCV as the video receiver for every camera: a video
               rewrite you asked not to do unless necessary. Not needed for on-demand
               audio of one camera at a time.
  OPTION C: go2rtc / MediaMTX -> new service; its low-latency audio path (WebRTC)
            does not pass through the Cloudflare Tunnel without a TURN server.
Chosen, exactly as your point 20 allows: audio ON DEMAND for the camera(s) someone
listens to -- never "video + audio connection for every camera":
  * one AudioWorker per camera, started by the first listener, shared by all
    listeners, kept 10 s after the last one (quick re-listen / mute grace), then closed;
    never kept in the background;
  * its RTSP session SETs UP ONLY THE AUDIO TRACK (the NVR sends audio, no video); if an
    NVR refuses that, it sets up video+audio on the same session and ignores the video;
  * it holds a normal NVR slot (key 2000 + camera), priority AUDIO (60) /
    AUDIO_FULLSCREEN (65) -- below every watched video (80-100): it can take a
    BACKGROUND stream's slot (the pool yields one at once) but NEVER a watched stream's;
    with no free slot the listener sees "Audio waiting for available NVR capacity";
  * at most CCTV_AUDIO_MAX_PER_NVR (2) audio sessions per NVR;
  * completely independent of the video workers (own thread, own reconnects).

4. DOES AUDIO REUSE THE VIDEO RTSP CONNECTION?                            NO (proven)
==========================================================================================
Not possible with OpenCV (rejects audio). Audio uses its own session -- but only for a
camera that someone is actually listening to, and only one per camera however many
browsers listen. Audio does not depend on the video quality: it always reads the
Standard stream's audio track (the same microphone as Original).

6. BROWSER DELIVERY PROTOCOL                                              PASS (browser)
==========================================================================================
WebSocket  ws(s)://<host>/audio/<index>?key=...  -- same access check as video (401
without the key, tested). Why not an HTTP stream: a grid page already uses all 6
HTTP/1.1 connections a browser allows per host (LAN / direct access); WebSockets do not
count against that limit. Cloudflare Tunnel supports WebSockets.
Messages: binary = [1, codec, packet number] + G.711 bytes as they arrive (real: 40 ms
= 320 bytes on NVR1, 128 ms = 1024 bytes on NVR2); text = {"state", "cause", "silent",
"codec", "rate"} on every change (no technical detail). Only NEW audio is sent.
Browser: decodes, plays through Web Audio with a ~0.25 s jitter buffer (held at
0.25-0.45 s by a ±2 % playback rate); audio more than 0.8 s behind live is dropped from
the QUEUE and playback re-anchored (never stale audio); pings every 15 s keep proxies
awake and detect dead connections.
Latency: server forwards each packet immediately (first packet 12 ms after connecting to
the fake NVR). Expected end-to-end: NVR packetising + network + ~0.25 s buffer, i.e.
roughly 0.3-0.6 s -- similar to or slightly ahead of the MJPEG video. NOT measured on
the real NVR (see 20).

14. RECONNECT RESULT                                                      PASS (offline)
==========================================================================================
NVR ends the audio stream: "RECONNECTING reason=STREAM_CLOSED" -> "PLAYING
reason=RECONNECTED"; no audio RTP PACKET for 8 s (= the video's read timeout; a silent
microphone still sends packets and never reconnects; a shorter stall shows "Audio
interrupted" and resumes on the same session): "RECONNECTING reason=READ_TIMEOUT" ->
PLAYING; PLAY accepted but no audio RTP at all: "NO_AUDIO_PACKETS" (with channel and
per-channel frame counts); the audio session cannot be opened: RECONNECTING / ERROR, retries with back-off,
recovers by itself. In all cases the listener's WebSocket stays open and the camera's
VIDEO was not touched (still LIVE, no transition, no reconnect). If the NVR is
unreachable, audio waits and reconnects with it (never plays stale audio: the browser
only plays packets as they arrive and drops late ones).

19. REAL AUDIBLE-CAMERA RESULT                 real stream PASS / heard: TO BE CONFIRMED
==========================================================================================
Superseded by REAL PRODUCTION above (live microphone audio delivered from six cameras
on your laptop server; whether it was heard is for you to confirm). Production (not
deployed yet) holds 6 connections per NVR; there the pool gives the audio session a
background slot. After deploying:
  1. open cctv.grav.in, open HR office (NVR2 ch8) fullscreen, click the speaker;
  2. you should hear the room; /api/status -> cameras[7].audio shows state PLAYING,
     codec PCMA, rtsp.nvrInterleaved {"rtp": 2}, levelDb (about -72 = G.711 digital
     silence / no microphone -> "no sound detected"; -60..-10 = sound);
  3. mute, change the volume, try another camera, switch Standard/Original.
If a camera plays but is silent (levelDb ~ -90), that camera has no microphone
connected or its audio is off in the NVR -- the NVR still sends a silent track.
