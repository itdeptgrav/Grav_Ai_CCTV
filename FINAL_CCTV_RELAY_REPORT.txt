1. CURRENT ARCHITECTURE FOUND
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One OpenCV/FFmpeg RTSP connection per camera, shared by all its viewers, but
ON-DEMAND: started by the first viewer, stopped the moment the last viewer leaves.
Start = RTSP pre-flight (auth DESCRIBE, ~3 s on a "cold" NVR2 channel) -> serialized
OpenCV open (~1.3 s) -> NVR slot (max 6 per NVR, taken after the open) -> frames.
Every page change, fullscreen return or reload paid the full handshake again. The
last JPEG was re-served for up to 5 s WITHOUT any marking (a 5-s-old picture looked
live). See CCTV_RELAY_ARCHITECTURE_REPORT.txt for the full analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. FINAL ARCHITECTURE IMPLEMENTED  (option C: HOT/WARM pool within the safe cap)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Browser A ─┐
Browser B ─┼─ MJPEG ── camera worker (1 per camera) ── ONE upstream RTSP ── NVR
Browser C ─┘                 │
                             └─ latest frame in RAM (JPEG + resized picture)

* PoolManager (server.py) runs 24/7 and decides which cameras are HOT, per NVR, up
  to NVR_CAP (6):  1. fullscreen viewers  2. grid / settings viewers (every viewed
  camera always keeps its worker)  3. recently viewed (15 min, newest first)
  4. background warming in grid order (page 1 first).
* A browser subscribes to a HOT camera's running stream: no RTSP handshake at all.
* Promotion: a viewed camera that is not HOT demotes the LEAST useful background
  stream (never a viewed one) and gets its slot; meanwhile the viewer sees the
  camera's cached frame at once, darkened and stamped "CACHED hh:mm:ss ·
  Connecting..." -- never passed off as live.
* Strict cap: in persistent mode the NVR slot is taken BEFORE connecting, so even
  during promotions/demotions a NVR never sees more than 6 streams from us.
  One worker thread per camera, always (a new one waits for the old one to end).
* Anti-thrash: immediate demotion only for a viewed camera's demand; otherwise at
  most one background swap per NVR per 30 s and never of a stream HOT < 2 min;
  warm-up 2 starts at a time per NVR (1 s apart); offline cameras leave the pool
  and are retried after 60 s, doubling up to once an hour; an unreachable NVR
  (reboot) is not treated as dead cameras -- its workers reconnect by themselves.
* Idle time: cameras outside the pool get their cached frame refreshed by a short
  visit (one per NVR per 5 min, only after 60 s without viewers on that NVR, and it
  only borrows a plain background slot, never a recently viewed camera's).
* Viewer arrival on an idle HOT camera switches it from 1 fps to 8 fps publishing
  immediately. JPEG is encoded once per published frame for all viewers.
* Fallback: CCTV_PERSISTENT=0 = the original on-demand behaviour (still covered by
  the original test suites). A worker that hits an unexpected error retries instead
  of dying.

9-11. DESIGN DETAILS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 9. RAM cache: per camera the latest JPEG (shared by all viewers), the resized
    picture, its monotonic + wall-clock time, and the time live video started on
    the current connection. Served as:
      LIVE    frame from the CURRENT connection and <= 2.5 s old
      CACHED  older frame (<= 30 min): darkened to ~40 % and stamped
              "CACHED hh:mm:ss" + state (Connecting / Waiting for NVR slot /
              Reconnecting / Camera offline); encoded once per frame+state
      STATUS  nothing usable: the status card
    RAM: ~0.7 MB per camera for the picture + ~30-60 KB JPEG (~20 MB for 25).
10. Stream reuse: one upstream per camera for any number of viewers; page changes
    and fullscreen only change subscriptions -- HOT workers keep running.
11. Main/sub stream: the pool uses the NVR SUB-stream (NVR2 H.265 1280x720
    ~1 Mbps, NVR1 H.264 352x288 0.3-1 Mbps). Main stream for fullscreen is NOT
    implemented in Phase 1 (it would cost a second slot per fullscreen camera).

21. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* 12 of 21 healthy cameras can be HOT at a time (cap 6 per NVR). Opening a non-HOT
  camera still costs its connect time (cached frame shown meanwhile).
* OpenCV opens are serialized process-wide (measured earlier: parallel opens are no
  faster), so several promotions at once still fill in one by one.
* The pool decodes its HOT streams continuously: steady CPU (section 17).
* Network: ~10 Mbps continuous NVR -> server for 12 HOT sub-streams. Fine on the
  LAN; if the production PC is NOT on the office LAN this is continuous upload --
  check the tunnel connector's origin IP (CCTV_RELAY_ARCHITECTURE_REPORT.txt).
* Run the pool on ONE server: production. Every other server.py against the same
  NVRs needs CCTV_PERSISTENT=0 (set on this laptop).
* A stalled (not closed) stream holds its slot until the 8 s read timeout.
* Refresh visits briefly (seconds) take one plain background camera out of the
  pool every 5 min per NVR; if someone opens exactly that camera then, it shows
  its cached frame and connects.

23. MEDIAMTX / GO2RTC / WEBRTC (PHASE 2/3 RECOMMENDATION)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not needed to make HOT cameras instant (Phase 1 does that). Worth it when CPU on
the production PC or latency matters: go2rtc (single binary) or MediaMTX relays
RTSP WITHOUT decoding (removes most of the ~26 %-of-a-core per NVR2 stream) and
offers WebRTC (sub-second start, low latency) and MJPEG. It does not raise the NVR
session limit. Plan: run it next to server.py, keep MJPEG as the fallback, switch
tiles to WebRTC only after it proves stable through the Cloudflare tunnel.
Cheaper first step: lower NVR2's sub-stream from 1280x720 H.265 to D1/CIF in the
NVR settings (your decision) -- decode cost drops several-fold.
